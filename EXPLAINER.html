The biological question

Mammals spent their first hundred million years in the dark. While dinosaurs occupied the daytime, early mammals were small and night-active, and that long confinement left permanent marks: a rod-dominated retina, the loss of two of the four ancestral cone opsins, elaborate whiskers, acute hearing, and a poor sense of colour relative to birds and reptiles. This is the nocturnal bottleneck hypothesis (Maor et al. 2017).

After the end-Cretaceous extinction, some lineages moved into daytime niches. Primates did. Ground squirrels did. Antelope, meerkats, cheetahs and diurnal rodents did. Crucially they did it separately, each from a night-active ancestor, at different times and on different continents.

That is a natural replicated experiment, and it is the experiment this project interrogates. If a shift to daytime activity requires particular changes in the circadian machinery, then lineages that made the shift independently should show those changes independently. Nature would have run the same trial ten times.

The question, stated precisely

Across ten independent origins of day-activity in mammals, do the same amino acid positions in the same circadian genes change to the same residues?

How the mammalian clock works, briefly

The eighteen genes tested are not an arbitrary panel. They constitute the core transcription-translation feedback loop that generates roughly 24-hour rhythms in nearly every mammalian cell.

The positive arm is a heterodimer of CLOCK (or its paralogue NPAS2) with ARNTL (BMAL1). This complex binds E-box elements and drives transcription. Among its targets are the genes of the negative arm, the Period family (PER1, PER2, PER3) and the Cryptochromes (CRY1, CRY2). PER and CRY proteins accumulate, dimerise, re-enter the nucleus and shut down the very complex that produced them. As they are degraded, repression lifts and the cycle restarts.

Three accessory sets tune that loop, and all are included here because a change in period or phase could plausibly arise in any of them:

  • Stabilising loop: NR1D1 and NR1D2 (REV-ERBα/β) repress ARNTL, while RORA, RORB and RORC activate it. Together they set the amplitude and stability of the oscillation.
  • Post-translational timing: CSNK1D and CSNK1E phosphorylate PER proteins and thereby set how fast they are degraded. These are the classic period-length genes; the tau mutation in hamsters sits in CSNK1E. FBXL3 targets CRY for degradation and does the same job on the other side.
  • Output and light response: BHLHE40 and BHLHE41 (DEC1/DEC2) are light-inducible repressors involved in resetting the clock after a light pulse.

If day-activity required a faster clock, a phase shift, or a different light-resetting response, the post-translational timing genes and the light-response genes are where one would most expect to see it. That expectation is testable and is one reason the panel spans all four functional modules rather than the core loop alone.

What convergence means here

The word is used loosely in the literature, and the whole analysis turns on being precise about it.

A naive comparison would mostly rediscover relatedness. All primates share thousands of residues with each other because they are primates. Since most diurnal mammals in any sample are phylogenetically clustered, a test that ignores the tree will report a large, entirely spurious signal. Several of the false positives encountered during this project were exactly that failure in different disguises.

The data

  • 60 mammal species, balanced 30 day-active to 30 night-active. The balance is deliberate for statistical power but is not representative: real mammals are predominantly nocturnal, and this matters (see below).
  • 18 core clock genes, protein alignments, one per gene.
  • 11,727 aligned amino acid positions after trimming poorly aligned regions. Each is an independent test.
  • Coding DNA for all 18 genes, matched by transcript accession and verified to translate exactly to the protein used. Coverage is 32 to 58 species per gene, lower than the protein data.
  • A dated species tree from Upham et al. 2019, used as a fixed scaffold so all genes share one evolutionary history.
A sampling caveat that already caused an error

The 30/30 balance is artificial. When ancestral states were first reconstructed with a model allowing different rates in each direction, it inferred a day-active ancestral placental mammal, inverting the premise of the study. The balanced sample had fed the model a false impression of how common day-activity is. The analysis now uses an equal-rates model, which fits better on this data and recovers a night-active ancestor without that assumption being imposed.

Finding the ten transitions

Nobody observed the ancestors, so the branches where activity pattern changed must be inferred. This is ancestral state reconstruction, done here with a continuous-time Markov model fitted across the whole tree, taking the state at each internal node with the highest marginal probability.

The result is 10 independent gains of day-activity and 5 reversions back to night-activity. Some gains are single species (Suricata suricatta, Acinonyx jubatus, Ochotona princeps, Octodon degus, Arvicanthis niloticus, Rhynchocyon petersi); others are whole radiations, the largest subtending 24 species.

Two consequences follow, and both shape the interpretation:

  • Gains and reversions are analysed as separate convergent classes. Merging them would be incoherent, since a method fitting a single shared signature cannot accommodate two opposite phenotypic directions at once; they would cancel.
  • The reconstructed transitions are read off the model, never hand-coded from the tips. A single species that is day-active within a night-active clade is a transition on its terminal branch; a day-active radiation is one transition deep in the tree, not many.

The six methods, and how each can fail

No method is trustworthy alone, so the same question was asked six ways, chosen because they fail differently.

PCOC — profile change on convergent branches
At any position, evolution tolerates only certain amino acids; that set of tolerances is a "profile". PCOC asks whether a position switched from one profile to a different, shared profile on the branches where day-activity arose. Failure mode: it fits one shared convergent profile, so it has power only when nearly all ten lineages converge together. Convergence in three of ten is effectively invisible to it.
Direct substitution counting — parsimony
Reconstruct each ancestor's residue, then count how often two or more day-active lineages independently changed to the same amino acid. No evolutionary model and no rate assumptions. Failure mode: the branches where transitions occur are real branches that carry more substitutions of every kind than random branches, which mechanically produces more same-residue coincidences. The test must condition on that.
Residue screen — model-free
For each column, is there an amino acid much commoner in day-active species than night-active ones? Failure mode: phylogenetic clustering. Corrected here by a null that re-places day-active clades of matched size at random on the same tree, preserving the clumping and randomising only the phenotype.
TDG09 — site-specific fitness shifts
Tests whether amino acid frequencies at a position differ between the two branch sets. Failure mode: any position whose residues track taxonomy will differ between the groups without convergence being involved. This turned out to matter enormously (see below).
RERconverge — relative evolutionary rate
Asks whether whole genes evolve faster or slower in day-active lineages, rather than examining individual positions. Failure mode: a gene-level average can hide a handful of important sites.
Contrast-FEL and RELAX — selection
Ask a genuinely different question: not whether the same residue recurred, but whether the strength or direction of natural selection differs on day-active branches. Selection can shift with no residue repeating at all. Failure mode: these need codon data and are sensitive to branches carrying little change.

Results, gene by gene

Headline

No convergent amino acid change is associated with the independent origins of day-activity, and no detectable shift in selective pressure either. Across roughly 27,000 position-level tests, five of six methods return nothing. The sixth returns so much that it cannot be believed.

Pooled across all genes: 648 positions had two or more day-active lineages change independently; 201 of those landed on the same residue. The observed rate is 0.310 against a chance expectation of 0.337. Marginally below chance, and in no gene meaningfully above it.

PCOC is even starker. Its posterior probability that a site is convergent never exceeds 0.098 anywhere in the dataset, against a detection threshold of 0.99. Fifteen of eighteen genes return exactly zero at every position.

Two genes cannot answer the question at all

CSNK1D shows zero positions with even two independent changes, and ARNTL shows two. Their branches carry so little evolutionary change that convergence could not have been detected there whatever the biology. Their nulls are uninformative rather than negative, and should be reported that way.

Gene-level rate and selection

RERconverge finds no gene significantly accelerated or decelerated in day-active lineages after correction for testing eighteen genes (all adjusted p > 0.67). The strongest is BHLHE40 at raw p = 0.051, with a negative correlation implying slightly stronger constraint in day-active lineages. Thirteen of eighteen genes trend in that same direction, which is suggestive of mildly increased purifying selection on the system as a whole, but is not individually significant.

Site-level selection testing across 15,349 codon positions finds no site with significantly different dN/dS between day-active branches and the rest. The lowest raw p-values (about 0.0002) are what 15,000 tests produce by chance.

The one method that disagreed, and why it lost

TDG09 flagged 885 of 3,820 tested positions, roughly a quarter of everything variable, spread across all eighteen genes. Taken at face value this would be a spectacular result.

It should not be taken at face value. A quarter of all variable sites showing phenotype-associated fitness shifts is not a plausible biological rate, and no other method corroborates a single one of the 885. The explanation is the confound described earlier: TDG09 asks whether amino acid frequencies differ between the two branch sets, day-active species are phylogenetically clustered, and so any position whose residues track taxonomy differs between the groups with no convergence involved.

Why this was anticipated

The project specified in advance that a site counts as high-confidence only when two or more independent methods flag it. That rule was written before any of these results existed, and it is what prevents 885 uncorroborated hits from becoming the headline. Applied here, the final list is empty.

Note also that correcting an earlier defect in how TDG09's inputs were built cut its hit rate substantially: CLOCK fell from 51 significant sites to 28. The fix removed a large artefact and left a large anomaly.

How far to trust this

A negative result is worth only as much as the demonstrated ability to detect a positive one. Two things were done about that.

Simulation on the real tree

A known convergent signal was simulated onto the actual tree at the actual transition branches, then the detector was asked to recover it. Power came back at essentially 1.0 with a false positive rate of 0 for all eighteen genes. That is reassuring but circular: the simulator generates data under the detector's own model using the detector's own machinery, so it cannot reveal a scenario that was constructed wrongly.

Planting signal in the real data

The stronger test implants artificial convergent sites into the real alignments and re-runs the entire pipeline blind. This exercises everything the simulation cannot: how transitions are matched onto each gene tree, how coordinates are mapped between file formats, how each tool's output is parsed.

Status — read before quoting any strength claim

The first attempt at this control was faulty. It wrote the artificial residue into 41 of 60 species rather than the intended 30, because the set of species descending from a converging lineage includes night-active species nested inside it. That made the planted residue the commonest amino acid in the tree, which is a new consensus rather than convergence, and the methods were right not to flag it. Every conclusion drawn from that run was withdrawn.

A corrected control is running now: 90 sites, planted only in day-active species, verified absent from all 30 night-active species, at three difficulty levels (all ten lineages converging, seven, and three). Its results are not yet available. Until they are, the direction of the result above is well supported but its strength is not established.

Ten defects, and what they have in common

This section exists because a collaborator reading the result deserves to know how it was produced. Over the course of the analysis, ten separate defects were found in the pipeline, the last of them in the tool built specifically to check for defects.

None of them produced an error message. Every one produced a confident, plausible, wrong number. Several sat in code that had already been reviewed. Among them:

  • An ancestral reconstruction that inferred a day-active ancestral mammal, inverting the study's premise.
  • Gains and reversions merged into one class, so opposite signals cancelled.
  • Convergent lineages described by their founding branch only, omitting the descendants that actually carry the residues.
  • Gene trees never re-rooted to match the species tree, so the founding branch of the largest day-active radiation was misidentified in all eighteen genes.
  • A tool taking the ancestral state from the order of two words on its command line, which had it assuming a day-active ancestor.
  • A parser reading a progress log rather than the results table, where every value is printed as zero.

All ten share one shape: an unchecked assumption that two representations of the same object agreed. The tree numbered one way in one program and differently in the next. A label present in memory but never written to the file. Alignment columns before pruning versus after.

They are fixed, and the pipeline now asserts these boundaries and refuses to proceed when they disagree; each assertion has been verified to fire on the original defect. But the honest summary remains: the direction of this result is well supported, and its precise strength is still being established.

What a null result would mean

It is not a failure, and it is biologically plausible. The core clock is among the most conserved systems in animals; essentially the same machinery runs in a mouse and a human, and the proteins are under strong purifying selection everywhere in the tree. Shifting activity into daylight may simply not require rewriting them.

The more likely mechanisms are ones this dataset cannot observe:

  • Regulatory change. Altering when and how strongly clock genes are expressed, rather than altering the proteins. This is the leading hypothesis and would leave coding sequence untouched.
  • Input pathway change. Retinal photoreception and the retinohypothalamic projection involve a different gene set entirely, including the melanopsin system.
  • Output pathway change. The same clock signal interpreted differently by downstream tissues, producing opposite behavioural phase with an unchanged oscillator.
  • Masking rather than entrainment. Some diurnal behaviour reflects direct behavioural responses to light rather than a re-phased clock at all.

The mild trend toward stronger constraint in day-active lineages (13 of 18 genes) is consistent with the idea that daytime species depend more tightly on precise clock function, since they rely more heavily on light-mediated entrainment. It is suggestive only.

What has not been tested

  • The five reversions to night-activity. A separate convergence test, not yet run. Power analysis indicates it would be interpretable, so this is a real gap rather than a hopeless one.
  • Robustness to the ancestral reconstruction model. The alternative model is retained as a sensitivity analysis and has not been swept.
  • Anything outside these 18 genes, and anything outside protein-coding sequence.
  • Gene expression, the leading candidate mechanism, for which no data exist here.
  • Two genes are uninformative even within scope (CSNK1D, ARNTL).

Glossary

Alignment column / site
One amino acid position, compared across all species. Column 47 in the cheetah is the same position as column 47 in the mouse.
Ancestral state reconstruction
Inferring what an unobserved ancestor was like, here whether it was day- or night-active, by fitting a model of how often the trait changes.
Transition branch
A branch of the tree on which the activity pattern changed. These ten branches, not the species, are the units of the experiment.
Homoplasy
Similarity not inherited from a common ancestor. Convergence is homoplasy that is associated with a trait; the rest is noise, and separating them is the central difficulty.
Purifying selection
Selection removing changes. Most positions in these proteins are under it, which is why so few positions vary at all.
dN/dS
Ratio of amino-acid-changing to silent substitutions. Below 1 indicates constraint; above 1 indicates positive selection. These genes sit far below 1.
False discovery rate
When thousands of positions are tested, some pass any threshold by chance. FDR correction adjusts for that; an uncorrected p-value across 15,000 tests means little.
